# Deferred decisions (ask user at end of goal)

Decisions made autonomously while driving toward ≥120 FPS demos, oracle parity, and hot-path exhaustion. Review at the end; none block continued work.

## Performance / product

1. **120 FPS scope:** Wall-clock `step()` ≤ 8.33 ms (p50) on Apple M3 Max Metal for interactive demos. Brick Ring / Brick Walls / Box Columns smash peaks are `stress` in `scene_fps` (275k+ contacts); still chase three-avbd GPU ms after accuracy.
2. **dt stays 1/60** for physics even when rendering at 120 Hz (two substeps or display interpolation later). Not changed unless oracle comparison requires it.
3. **One mid-step GPU sync** after coloring to read `color_count` / `color_counts` and skip empty solve colors. Preferable to 200+ empty dispatches; revisit with Metal indirect command buffers later.
4. **CSR constraint gather** is mandatory for solve/color; brute-force contact scans are treated as bugs, not a valid mode.
5. **Pairwise broadphase** kept for ≤512 colliders; grid above that. Threshold may need tuning per scene density.
6. **Capsule ropes** may keep sphere-chain demos until capsule–capsule + long-chain AVBD is stable; accuracy tests should cover both when ready.

## Vehicles (`slang/kernels/vehicle_step.slang`, `crates/engine-vehicle`)

v1. **Raycast wheels as rows on the chassis, not bodies.** Suspension, bump stop, anti-roll, tyre friction, pitch/roll limit and aero act only on the chassis, so a vehicle adds no CSR/colour edges and costs one body. `vehicle_prepare`/`vehicle_iterate` write a 6×6 block into the `gAabbs` tail; `primalAccumulate` adds it for `BODY_VEHICLE` bodies (block index in `BodyState.initialPad`). Precomputing is exact: nothing else moves the chassis between the vehicle pass and its primal. The hot primal kernel keeps its 10 WGSL storage bindings.

v2. **Wheel spin solved exactly per iteration.** A first version condensed the spin DoF with a fixed Schur complement and back-substituted linearly. With a saturated tyre the coupling stiffness `R²k` against `I/dt²` made convergence ~2.5%/iteration (launch traction at half the expected value). Dropping the stiffness instead let the light wheel overshoot. Each wheel now solves its 1-D monotone piecewise-linear equation exactly (tyre and brake limits included) and contributes the Schur complement of the regime it lands in.

v3. **Friction bound uses the curve's falling branch.** Jolt curves give μ(0) = 0; with step-start slip a tyre that started slipping mid-step had no grip for a step. The bound is `μ(max(slip, slip_peak))`; the AVBD penalty supplies the elastic region. Stick anchors carried across steps hold parked cars on a 12° slope with zero measured drift.

v4. **Destruction-ready, not destruction.** Parts own chassis colliders; a part is attached iff its colliders belong to the chassis, so a fracture system can sever with `CMD_REPARENT` alone. Severed wheels/parts activate parked bodies on the GPU (point velocity, wheel spin, analytic mass/inertia removal) with a collision grace via `Collider.generation` (= ignored body + 1). `MountDesc` is reserved; nothing breaks automatically yet.

v5. **Large statics and large-vs-small-static broadphase.** Statics wider than a cell are listed (scratch 53..63, reset in `broadphase_clear` because Metal does not rewrite GPU-owned scratch words); past 10 the legacy endpoint probe applies. Large dynamics (vehicle chassis) now walk the grid cells under their AABB for small statics, which never query. `broadphase_clear` also resets scratch words 25/42 (the large-dynamic and plane lists), which Metal never rewrote between steps.

v6. **Wheel queries walk the broadphase grid.** They are dispatched right after `broadphase_query`, while the cell heads and links still describe x⁻ (the contact hash and CSR reuse that scratch later). Each 64-lane workgroup walks the cells its segment crosses, plus planes, large dynamics and listed large statics. The original full scan stays behind a header flag as the oracle; the two match bit for bit on an obstacle course. Query rates (Jolt) skip probes, staggered per wheel, and slide the contact along the last hit plane, which is stored in ground-body space.

v7. **Tracks: spin groups, box friction, and a steering brake.** Every wheel of a track shares the leader's spin y, so the exact 1-D solve sums the group's rows, and the Schur complement becomes `Σ k JᵀJ − u uᵀ/A`. Jolt's left/right sync alone does not steer here: an implicit tyre row returns a synced track to ground speed within the iteration loop. So the inner track gets a bounded servo toward `ratio × outer` speed, and its reaction torque drives the outer track (a regenerative steering differential, without which steering from a standstill only braked). Two settings keep pivots and fast turns sane:
* Box friction, so drive force does not eat scrub capacity.
* A lateral-g ratio cap, so a fixed 0.6 ratio does not spin a 20 m/s tank out.

Jolt's lateral friction of 2 left the pivot on the stick/slip edge: it stalled at 8 iterations and spun at 10. The tank preset uses 1.

v8. **Motorcycle lean needs Jolt's linear compensation.** An angular-only lean row rotates the chassis about its COM, dragging both contact patches sideways. The resulting lateral friction enters the next target lean, and the bike oscillated into a slide on any steer input. Adding the linear force `−m·(Δω × r̄)`, with r̄ the load-weighted contact offset, makes it roll about the tyres; a left steer then settles into a steady lean.

v9. **Wheel loads are capped and wheels ignore dynamic obstacles they could not climb (found by play-testing).** A monster truck driving into a stack of 40 kg crates was thrown 300+ m: a crate inside a wheel's sphere cast read as full compression, and the stiff bump stop answered with 0.7–1.25 MN on one wheel (normal loads are 5–10 kN). The ground body is held fixed during the step, so the crate also received hundreds of m/s. Three changes, now covered by a regression test:
* Each wheel's normal load is capped at `bump_max_force`, else 8× an even share of the weight over the attached wheels. Past the cap the row is a constant force.
* The velocity change a wheel's reaction gives a dynamic ground body is capped at their relative speed plus 2 m/s.
* Ray and sphere hits on dynamic bodies nearer than the wheel could climb (centre at full bump) are ignored, so a crate under a wheel is left to the chassis contacts instead of shoving that corner up. Static ground keeps the bump stop for hard landings.

Rollovers in the 49-vehicle traffic scene dropped from about 6 to 4 in 12 s, and lone vehicles circling at constant input never roll.

v10. **A saturated tyre shares its friction ellipse along the slip velocity (combined slip).** The budget used to be split in proportion to the raw row forces. The lateral row is stiff, so a handbrake-locked rear wheel kept nearly all its side grip: the turn tightened only from 33° to 35° in 1.2 s. The split now follows (κ, tan α), the two slip velocities over |v_long|, and the AVBD dual update re-clamps to that share rather than the full lateral limit. A locked rear now kicks out (69° against 33° in 1 s, covered by a test), and a wheel spinning under power loses side grip as it should. Play-testing also showed that instant full keyboard lock at speed asked for about 2 g. Road-car presets therefore scale steering with speed (`Curve::road_steering`: full lock to 8 m/s, a quarter at 60 m/s). The destructible sedan's centre of mass is shifted back 0.25 m to match the sedan's balance, because its separable 250 kg engine sits at the nose.

v11. **Combined slip blends in only past the grip peak; traction control; arcade tank input (play-testing).**
* **Combined slip.** Splitting a saturated tyre's ellipse purely along the slip direction stripped a driven tyre's side grip at κ ≈ 0.05 on a straight. A motorcycle swapped ends under full throttle after any turn. The split now blends from the raw-force split at the peak to the slip direction at twice the peak slip (Pacejka-style normalisation by κ̂ and α̂), so the handbrake still kicks the tail out.
* **Traction control** (`assists.traction_control`, on for the sedans and the motorcycle). Throttle scales down from 0.8× to 1.6× peak slip. The slip ratio is regularised as slip speed / max(|v|, 3 m/s), since κ blows up at a standstill and choked launches. The cut is low-passed (τ 0.1 s, `V_QUERY.y`), because applied raw it chattered at the threshold and amplified Metal vs WebGPU rounding into a 7% launch divergence. Below the top gears, the motorcycle's engine delivers about twice the rear tyre's grip.
* **Motorcycle aero centre** is now at the centre of mass. Applied above it, drag at speed unloaded the front tyre.
* **Tank input** is now an arcade skid-steer mix (left = throttle + k·steer, right = throttle − k·steer). The old Jolt-sample mapping did nothing when steering without throttle above the pivot speed, and inverted the yaw sense in reverse. `turn_ratio` and `pivot_speed` are replaced by `steer_strength`. Jolt's opposite-sign track sync sends two equal track speeds to 0/0, which "satisfies" any ratio, so a partial counter-rotation from rolling never turned. Counter-rotating ratios now use the least inertia-weighted projection onto right·ωl = left·ωr. For tanks the reverse-brake rule is skipped while steering, because a pivot swings the hull's forward speed negative.

v12. **Two-way ground coupling: two-body tyre rows plus wheel shells (Jolt's model, in AVBD).** Ray-cast wheels used to treat the ground as fixed during the step and push back on dynamic bodies afterwards, as a lagged and capped velocity change. Debris didn't react properly, a deck never felt the vehicle's load in-step, and three workarounds (wheel-load cap, reaction cap, ignoring unclimbable hits) stood in for physics. Jolt solves suspension, max-up stop and friction as two-body constraint parts between the vehicle and each wheel's contact body, linked into one island (`VehicleConstraint::BuildIslands`, Wheel.cpp). The AVBD equivalent:
* `vehicle_link` points a per-wheel `JOINT_TYRE` joint at (chassis, ground) when the ground is dynamic, so the pair is coloured apart and the ground body's primal gathers the wheel.
* Every tyre row adds its ground Jacobian (−[e; r_g × e], same stiffness and force as the chassis side) to a per-wheel ground block. The chassis rows see the ground's current iterate instead of a given motion.
* Static ground keeps the old unary fast path with no graph cost.
* Tyre joints are skipped by the joint pair filter, warm-starting and the joint dual update.

Wheel shells cover side contact: five spheres per tyre on the chassis, at 60°…300° from straight down, following steer and suspension. They are a geometric version of PhysX's wheel shapes plus contact modification. A sphere-cast hit belongs to the tyre rows only inside a 35° cone from the suspension axis. For hits above the full-bump position, the cone is measured from the full-bump wheel centre, so a crate edge in the wheel arch goes to the shell and chassis rather than to a suspension compressed past its stop.

Result: the three workarounds are gone. A car accelerating on a slab that slides on ice conserves momentum within 1.4%, a parked car tips a seesaw plank, and the crate-stack crash shoves crates instead of launching the truck. Peak wheel loads while ramming rigid crates stay large (a 3 t truck losing about 3 m/s in about 15 ms is about 600 kN), so the tests bound motion, not force.

Host notes:
* WgpuSim and the JS host previously never dispatched the joint graph passes; they do now.
* Capacity reserves one joint per wheel slot, and the AABB tail gains a ground block per wheel slot.
* A partial `float3` store and a constant-folded quaternion both tripped naga's SPIR-V front end; the shell code uses whole-record stores and separate park and place paths.
* Tyre joints never enter the joint pair hash, so with no user joints the hash is cleared once instead of every step (64k entries).
* Cost at traffic scale (48 vehicles, 400 crates, WgpuSim on an M3 Max): 8.77 ms/step against 8.44 ms before coupling (+4%). This is mostly the shell spheres in the broadphase and the real crate coupling.

v13. **A cast that starts inside a collider reports where it entered, and the tyre cone filters each collider (no invented full-bump hits).** Three monster-truck reports had one cause: a wheel's sphere cast starts at the mount, and a sphere of tyre radius there often overlaps a crate, a kerb or a ramp side that the tyre itself never touches. The cast primitives returned t = 0 with n = −d for any start inside a shape, so the hit read as full compression straight along the suspension axis and passed the 35° cone. The 6 Hz bump stop answered as if the crate were under the tyre:
* **Wedge load.** Ramming the crate stack gave 1.77 MN on one wheel, 59.7× the truck's weight. It was not the rigid-crate deceleration v12 assumed: every spike had t = 0 and invCos = 1.
* **Traffic hops.** In Chrome the traffic take threw monster trucks up to 3.8 m above their ride height (y 5.6).
* **monster_stack take.** The truck was kicked sideways in the stack and ended on its side against the ramp.

Changes:
* Sphere, box, rounded box (sphere sweep), capsule and plane casts from an inside origin report the entry behind the origin (t ≤ 0) and the real surface normal there. `raycast_batch` still reports a ray starting inside a collider as a hit at its origin.
* The tyre cone (and the full-bump test for hits above L_min) moves from the single nearest hit into the per-collider scan (`tyreAccepts`). A side hit is the shell's, and it no longer hides the ground or crate top further down the travel. Grid walk and full scan share the test, so they stay bit-identical.

Results:
* Crate-stack ram: peak wheel load 59.7× → 7.8× weight, chassis max y 4.06 → 1.92 m. The 7.8× that remains is real: a rear tyre climbs a 1 m crate edge at 9.6 m/s, with a 30° normal and 4.5 m/s into the surface. The bump damper (D ≈ 39 kN·s/m) plus 4 cm of bump spring give ≈ 220 kN against 231 kN measured. The test now bounds the load at 12× weight and the height at 3 m.
* A new test parks the truck beside a ledge inside its mount sphere but 0.9 m from the wheel centre. It fails on the old casts at step 0 (138 kN, the truck kicked 0.5 m sideways); with the fix the tyre stays on the ground.
* Traffic take in Chrome: the worst hop is now 1.18 m against 3.84 m. The rest is quasi-static: trucks high-centred on crate piles, vertical speed ≤ 1.2 m/s.
* monster_stack take: the truck ploughs through the stack upright. With clean contacts it now carries 10 m/s into the scripted full-lock turn, where it slides at ~0.95 g. Its static stability factor is half-track/CG = 1.45/1.58 ≈ 0.92 g, so rubble under a tyre can trip it over, which is physical. On flat asphalt it does not roll at any speed up to 10 m/s (`trace_rollover_threshold`). The take turns at 7 m/s (~0.75 g).
