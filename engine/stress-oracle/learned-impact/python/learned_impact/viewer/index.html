How to read this replay and its limits

Chunk color is the largest peak load / strength ratio of its attached live bonds in the selected interval. Blue is lightly loaded, amber is near the strength limit, and red is at or above it. This is interval peak utilization, not an instantaneous stress tensor. White outlines highlight pieces attached to bonds that break in this interval; small marks locate bond ends where visible. A colored chunk is not itself a failure label. In the break comparison, a piece touching several error types shows missed first, then extra, then caught.

Playback interpolates positions and rigid rotations between verified oracle snapshots. Stress and break labels stay on their recorded interval. In Model endpoint mode, the right panel interpolates from each recorded start pose to the learned world endpoint, then resets to the next oracle start. Projectiles and active-piece masks still follow the oracle. The two panels do not simulate independent collapse trajectories. The model is evaluated again from each oracle frame, so a missed break does not keep fragments attached in the right panel. Local displacement can reset when the oracle splits a cluster; these intervals are excluded from the new model’s displacement loss when cluster history is available. Amber dots on blast scenarios mark the recorded source, not a predicted shock front.
