## Oracle quality issue requiring source repair

A read-only audit of all 272 recordings found a catastrophic finite-valued
trajectory in validation event `v4_layout_09_c3_s101`. At interval 1 (16.7–33.3 ms),
chunk 749 reaches 48.7 million m/s and 33.1 billion rad/s. Mechanical energy jumps
from 0.923 MJ to 4.287e18 J; the scene has no prescribed loads or scheduled events.
This one event contributes 99.999982% of validation position-residual squared
error. Other train/test maxima do not show a comparable spike. Binary/replay
identity checks can reproduce this trajectory exactly while still admitting bad
physics; finite-value checks are insufficient.

The source simulation needs investigation and repair before the corpus can be
described as physically validated. Existing partitions and reported scores are
retained: no outlier is silently removed and no target is clipped. Validation
fracture F1 also includes this event, so prior checkpoint selection remains
provisional. A contact energy ledger can be signed; its negative value alone is
not an admissibility criterion. The simultaneous unforced energy/speed explosion
is the concrete issue. Evidence: `runs/world-quality-v10-2026-10-19/{audit,energy-followup}.json`.

The substep trace localizes the first excursion to a contact between chunks 748
and 749 at 33.0256 ms. After crushing, signed tetrahedral volumes sum to exactly
zero in rational arithmetic, but division of each term before summation leaves
9.96e-60 cubic metres. The residual first moment then produces a contact point
32 km away. `Polytope::mass_properties` now accumulates unscaled determinants and
applies the integration constants after summation. A self-contained two-fragment
regression fails before this change and passes after it; separate tests retain
positive volumes down to 8e-48 cubic metres without a cutoff. No contact-force
cap, geometry simplification or timestep change is involved. Full-impact
regeneration of the failing scene now reaches 25.58 m/s maximum speed, 83.40 rad/s
maximum spin and 15.64 mm constant-velocity position residual RMSE (previously
104.64 m). Its recorded mechanical energy never exceeds its initial value. Two
comparison scenes also remain below their initial energy. All three saved scenes
and numerical settings match their sources exactly. Evidence is under
`runs/oracle-instability-v10-2026-10-19/`; the original corpus and checkpoints
remain unchanged.

A fresh 290-scenario / 3,472-interval rebuild is running under
`runs/fixed-oracle-v10-2026-10-19/`, retaining every original split and separating
the magnitude-pilot cohort in metadata. The binary and Rust/Python sources are
fingerprinted. Each event must preserve its saved scene/settings, validate finite
arrays, world targets, forcing descriptors, exact joint history and material
mapping. A combined manifest is withheld until collection succeeds; complete

### What is now saved

Each event retains all schema arrays: mass/inertia/supports, topology, bond geometry,
stiffness/strength/fracture energy/rebar, initial joint and chunk state, four load
bins, contact flags, prior/QS force, failure class/mode, peak utilization, end joint
state/force, and state delta. Additional sidecars contain:

- `scene.json`: full scene, materials, projectiles, loads, solver settings, and seed.
- `context.json`: stable chunk/bond keys, initial geometry and bond frames, actual
  QS-probe setting, frame basis, recorder revision, and exact generator parameters.
- `replay.json`: initial plus every end-of-interval world pose, activity, cluster IDs,
  world linear/angular velocities, projectile motion, blast source, and mechanical/
  dissipated energy. Collection stamps the event fingerprint.

Load vectors now use the **frame-start cluster basis**. Local deformation coordinates
may reset during fragmentation; recorded cluster IDs let training exclude those
discontinuous delta targets. The fixed-graph recorder fails explicitly if refinement
introduces uncaptured active chunks/bonds. It must not silently omit their labels.
Historical replay verification preserves the old end-frame load convention.

Exact scene data preserves constitutive parameters not yet consumed by the model
(such as friction, rate-law coefficients and fatigue settings). The old packed
22-field joint schema still encodes infinite ductility as zero and caps large finite
ductility; a future full-state schema should distinguish those cases. This recording
format is not a complete solver checkpoint or a ready-made causal contact model.

### Remaining gaps, in priority order

1. **Duration:** 52/257 events are still breaking in their final interval (17/80 new
   events). House v3 clips are 24 ms; wall/masonry clips are about 133 ms. Extend the
   active tails, then capture settling, secondary impact, and repeated loads.
2. **Independent geometry:** most data reuse the same wall/house topology. Vary wall
   thickness/aspect ratio, opening sizes/locations, roof mass, supports, missing
   members, and connectivity. Hold out complete layouts, not just parameter values.
3. **Graph scale:** 2…2,016 chunks currently; no 10,000-body training or coupled
   validation. Replication invariance of attention is tested at 10,000 nodes, which
   is not a substitute for larger connected structures or engine benchmarks.
4. **Material/loads:** mixed materials, rebar layouts, friction/rate/fatigue variation,
   off-axis moments/spin, multiple impacts, blast directions/shielding and repeated
   blast/impact sequences. Balance factor combinations and include true negatives.
5. **Feedback:** interval contact forces are currently collected after oracle stepping.

## Experimental causal input recordings

`learned_impact_permute --rigid-preview` also records two optional arrays per frame:
`chunk_preview_load_bins[N,4,6]` and `chunk_preview_contact[N]`. The versioned
`context.load_preview` contract is required; both Rust and Python reject incomplete,
nonfinite, incorrectly shaped, or unversioned preview data. Ordinary recordings keep
all existing arrays and behavior. Resume refuses a changed preview contract and
regenerates valid-but-truncated clips instead of accepting them as complete jobs.

The preview starts from a detached copy of the oracle's **starting** state. It
retains the existing contact solver and substep size, freezes local elastic
geometry/velocity, and integrates cluster/projectile rigid motion and scripted
loads without evaluating dynamic bond responses. This is an explicit experimental
approximation for **inputs only**. The oracle still performs the full, unchanged
stress/contact calculation and produces all labels and displayed motion. Coupled,
non-explicit, and adaptive-refinement worlds are rejected by the preview method.

`physics-preview-v3` uses only these preview force bins/contact flags, with no
fallback to future oracle force bins. Initial state, prior damage, and graph are
still supplied by the oracle at every interval: this does not establish autonomous
rollout quality. Frozen deformation can substantially change contact forces; measure
that error and the cost before deciding whether this interface belongs in the engine.
`timing.json` saves separate per-frame oracle and preview wall times (including clone
cost and concurrent-job contention, excluding recording/IO).

New `scene.json` files explicitly pin **all solver method switches**, including
those normally omitted by the generic scene serializer. `context.solver_methods`
and the recipe catalog preserve the actual settings as well. This prevents
`STRESS_METHODS` or later default changes from silently changing a replay. Older
files without this field still need exact replay verification.
