# P0 step 5 pilot: learnability and data scaling, variants A and B (2026-10-21)

Data: P1's calls `/3` (recorder rev 6, builder rev 2), free walls only.
- **Training:** nested subsets of 55 / 110 / all 173 recipe groups, which is about 64 / 131 / 208 events (≤ 10,644 calls).
- **Validation:** 3,135 calls from 67 events, the same for every run, including 14 complete 8-member ensembles.
- **Gates:** active calls only (impact or secondary contact, or any member breaking), with the oracle-calibrated skill rule.

Model: W32, rounds 2/1/2/1/2, 600–2,000 steps (9 per event), pushforward 0.3, 3 seeds per point, MPS.
- Table and fits: `runs/v3/pilot-step5/pilot.md` (not committed: `runs/` is ignored).
- Reproduce with `python -m learned_impact.outcome.pilot` (command at the end).

**This is a pilot on ≤ 208 events of one family, with short training. The projections are rough, and nothing here is an accuracy claim.**

## Results (mean over 3 seeds [min, max]; gate: skill ÷ bar ≤ 1)

| Metric | A, N≈64 | A, N≈131 | A, N≈208 | B, N≈64 | B, N≈131 | B, N≈208 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ram exit speed | 1.16 [1.01, 1.45] | 0.95 [0.76, 1.23] | 1.23 [1.14, 1.33] | 0.91 [0.55, 1.09] | 0.58 [0.43, 0.66] | **0.51** [0.39, 0.67] |
| partition (mass-weighted ARI) | 1.00 | 0.98 | 0.95 | 1.00 | 1.02 | 0.98 |
| fragment sizes (W1 on log mass) | 2.59 | 2.27 | 2.24 | 2.27 | 2.69 | 2.35 |
| bond recall | 0.16 | 0.34 | 0.34 | 0.49 | 0.14 | 0.32 |
| false-break rate (no-break calls) | 0.1% | 0.1% | 0.0% | 0.5% | 0.1% | 0.2% |

Three-way report on the same calls:
- **Superiority** (beats `blast_parity` QS *and* the CPU punch on every primary metric): A in 8 of 9 runs, B in 5 of 9.
- **Non-inferiority** on the calls QS already gets right (aggregate within the gate tolerances): passes in every run.
  - Per call, A is not worse on 98–99.8% of them.
  - B is not worse on 90.5–99.9%. Its failures are extra breaks where QS predicts none.

## What it says

1. **The problem is learnable on ram speed, and variant B learns it with little data.**
   - B passes the calibrated ram gate from about 131 events (0.51 × bar at 208) and keeps improving with data (fit exponent 1.9).
   - A hovers around the bar (0.95–1.23) with no clear trend.
   - The QS prior gives B the impulse scale for free. This matches the plan's expectation that B is the most data-efficient.
2. **Partition sits at the bar for both,** and improves only slowly with data.
3. **Fragment sizes are the blocker: 2.2–2.7 × bar, flat in data for both variants.**
   - A's fit has its asymptote at 2.2, above the bar.
   - By P0 step 5's own rule ("if the curve is flat, change the features or the boundary before generating more"), more data alone is not the next step for sizes.
   - The cause is visible in bond recall (≈ 0.3): the model misses most breaks, so its fragments are too few and too large.
   - The closed-loop videos show the same thing: 34 pieces against the oracle's 50 on a 60 m/s val wall.
   - On the M5 symmetric free wall it over-breaks instead (38 pieces against 6), which is outside what these walls taught it.
4. **The data does not limit yet; the break decision does.** The output-side ceiling showed the decode can represent every break exactly (the failure margin misclassifies 0 of 5,140 val breaks), so the shortfall is the model, not the boundary.

## Next, in order (each a validation-only change, measured on this same pilot)

1. **The break decision's operating point:**
   - fit τ on the margin (the plan allows τ to be tuned on validation);
   - the margin loss weight and the consequence weights on cut bonds;
   - training length (runs stop at ≤ 2,000 steps; check that validation is not still improving).
2. **Capacity:** the W64 / 12-round reference on the same data.
3. **Variant B by default for the rest of P2** (A stays as the comparison).
4. **Then more data:** P1's bulk 1k / 4k points, once the size curve moves.

Training throughput on these calls (MPS, after shape bucketing and removing per-step syncs): about 66 ms per step of about 37 calls, about 550 calls/s. A 30k-event epoch would take about 9 minutes.
