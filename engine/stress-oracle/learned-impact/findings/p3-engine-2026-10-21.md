# P3 engine side of the learned impact model: the stub path runs (2026-10-21)

`STRESS_IMPACT_PUNCH=learned` (off by default) in stress-gpu's resident frame, built so that
when P2's weights and P0's Slang network arrive only the network kernel needs plugging in.
Code: `crates/stress-gpu/shaders/learned.slang`, `src/learned.rs`, the hooks in
`src/world/resident_frame.rs`, `src/solver.rs` (`FrameSplits::hook`, `post_split`),
`src/gpu_pass.rs` (`PassHook`). Until the network is wired a stand-in must be chosen
explicitly: `STRESS_LEARNED_STUB=zero|punch|nonfinite` (without it the frame fails with
a clear error).

## What runs, and where

P0's validated boundary (step 3 of the feasibility note): one call per routed impact island
per frame, inputs the frame-start state, applied at window end with the island's contacts
filtered. In the resident frame, at the punch's place (the end of frame N, after the
cascade), all in the frame's one submission:

1. **Window end of the batch made at the end of N−1** (`post`, before the split pass):
   `learned_check` (the call's islands still as it saw them, else marked stale and not
   applied), `learned_apply_bonds` (breaks as the punch leaves a broken bond's record,
   survivors' damage raised to the decoded value and kept below 1, broken islands marked
   disconnected), `learned_apply_bodies` / `_impactors` (bodies released; impactors and
   unsplit islands placed at start + dx, turned by drot, with the momentum-exact fit: stress-ref
   `apply_decoded`'s rule).
2. **The split pass** with `learned_place` after its spawn: each child of a call's island
   placed and fitted from its own chunks' decoded motion, before the pass's log (so the host
   adopts the placed bodies).
3. **Routing check, then the new batch** (`post_split`): `learned_select` (the punch's sweep
   per island; impactors as a mask), `learned_debris` (`STRESS_DEBRIS_ROUTING=1`: islands the
   launched debris reaches, the host routing's 2(v_imp − v_cl)dt bound), `learned_count_bonds`,
   `learned_compact` (calls = impactors joined by shared islands, in impactor order; islands
   in island order; nodes = chunks then impactors; capacities checked), the gathers
   (frame-start state of chunks, impactors, live bonds), `learned_finish` (centre of mass,
   scales L, m, v, dt, E_in as the calls schema defines them; freedom), **the network or the
   stub**, `learned_decode`, `learned_gate`, `learned_tally`.
4. **Hold**: a call that stands has its islands' bodies and impactors made static for frame
   N+1 (inverse mass and inertia 0, velocity 0: the same hold `RubbleEngine::step` uses for
   island bodies), so the engine neither moves them nor exchanges contact between them: the
   call's contacts are filtered. Their state is kept and restored at step 1 of the next frame.
5. **Fallback** (explicit, logged, counted): a call whose decode is non-finite or whose KE
   bound is infeasible (rigid motion alone exceeds the bound) is not held or applied; its
   islands go to the GPU punch, which runs right after the gate with the islands a standing
   call took skipped (`punch.slang` `LEARNED_BIT`, never set otherwise), then the split
   pass once more. The host prints each fallback with its reasons and keeps totals
   (`Learned::totals`).

The decode (`learned_decode`) is P2's `learned_impact.outcome.decode` in f32: to_physical,
the exact momentum projection of a causally free call (no anchored chunk, no pressure load,
no gravity: P2's current `causal_freedom`), the KE bound `KE_out ≤ KE_in + W_g + tol·E_in`
(`STRESS_LEARNED_ENERGY_TOL`, default 0.05; a free call scales only its non-rigid motion, so
P and L stay exact), breaks at `log q̂ ≥ log τ`, damage below 1.

## The input features on the GPU (2026-10-22)

`shaders/learned_feat.slang`, `src/learned_feat.rs`: the calls schema `/3` arrays for every
call of the batch, in the same submission, between the gathers and the network:

- **static per-key tables**, uploaded once from the world's pristine structures and scene
  (P1's `Statics`: scaled modulus, density, strengths, G_f·A, Weibull factor, rest rotation,
  centre, stiffnesses, bond geometry), an incidence list and a sorted bond lookup by stable
  chunk rows; a layout bond finds its row by its chunks' keys each batch;
- **predicted impactor contacts** (stress-ref `predict_contacts`, min speed 0): swept bounds,
  struck chunk, the punch's plug (cone + bonded), Hertz duration and peak force, plug ratio;
- **scales** c, f_t, E, GA and the median bond area (medians as the builder's), the
  reference speed with the contacts' closing speeds;
- **node features** (damage summaries over each chunk's pristine bonds, hops to contact and
  to anchor, wave arrival by relaxation, plug and contact flags), **impactor** and **global**
  features, **bond** features (failure margin from the joint state, see below), and the
  **impactor-contact, cracked-face and structure-contact** edge sets;
- columns placed through `ColumnMap` from the schema's lists (`schema_columns()`, checked
  equal to stress-learned's `feature_columns()` in the test): a schema change moves columns
  without touching the kernels, and a column the kernels do not compute is an error;
- every kernel fits WebGPU's 10 storage buffers (`binding_limits.rs`); 64 threads per group.

**Verified against P1's Rust call builder** (`stress-rubble/tests/learned_features.rs`):
every routed frame's batch is exported (`Learned::export`) and `outcome_calls::live_call`
runs on the world as the GPU left it, for the same keys (`STRESS_LEARNED_DRY=1`: batches
never stand, so the state after the frame is the batch's). Scenes: the free wall and the
wall row (`*_late2`, the impact point moved ±0.5–0.9 mm across the face as the oracle
ensembles do: the unperturbed scenes are exactly symmetric, a tie f32 and f64 break
differently) and **P1's generator's first evaluation members** of the free and anchored
wall families (pilot ranges, seed 101; their strike moved two frames later, since the GPU
world's first frame is coupled). **11 calls: identical edge sets of all four types, every
column within 2e-3 relative (most within 1e-6), on Metal and on WGSL.**

Columns not compared (documented; their source is a history the engine does not keep):
- `bond_feat.fail_margin`: the oracle keeps a running maximum over its joint evaluations;
  the engine reconstructs it from the joint state (κ over κ_fail per family, rebar work);
- `bond_feat.log1p_peak_util_prev`: the engine writes the frame-end utilisation;
- `bond_feat.log_qs_hertz_util`: the converged Hertz-peak QS prior is not computed (0);
- `cracked_feat.frames_since_broken`: the engine stamps a bond when a batch first sees it
  broken.

**Found on the way: a Slang code-generation hazard.** A group reduction written as
`r = shared[0]; barrier; return r;` came out of Slang as `barrier; return shared[0];`: the
read moved past the barrier, so a fast SIMD-group could start the next reduction and
overwrite the array before a slower one had read it (seen as entry chunks and bounds that
differed between SIMD-groups, intermittently, more often under load). Every reduction in
`learned*.slang` now starts with a barrier, and medians are kept in their own words. Other
shaders that read a reduction's result and then reuse the array may have the same race
(not audited here).

## Kept deformation, the window-end energy check, cost estimates (2026-10-22)

**Kept per-chunk deformation** (`STRESS_DECODED_DEFORMATION=1`, P0's boundary choice:
free walls pass the gate only with it). `feat_deform`, after the split pass of a frame whose
batch was applied, writes each chunk's decoded departure from its body's fitted rigid
motion into its hidden state `state[4c..4c+3]`: displacement and rotation in the body frame,
velocity and spin against the body's (an anchored body's: against its spin only), as
stress-ref `apply_decoded_with(keep_deformation)` defines them. The gather takes each
chunk's own orientation and velocity with its hidden rotation and velocity, so the next
call sees the carried state. `tests/learned_gpu_deform.rs` (stub `punch-wobble`: pieces
that do not move rigidly): after each window end every chunk sits at its frame-start
position plus its decoded displacement, worst 6e-8 m, with up to 2.3 mm kept as hidden
displacement. Caveat: a frame in which the island is not routed runs the quasi-static
cascade on it, whose static solve replaces the hidden displacement with its equilibrium
(the hand-back to QS); the carried state survives only while calls continue.

**Window-end energy** (`tests/learned_gpu_energy.rs`, P1's anchored-wall evaluation member
under gravity on the ground): the bodies' kinetic plus gravity energy after the frame that
follows a window end, against before it. Punch stub and an adversarial stub
(`punch-overlap`: the ram placed 5 mm into the plug ahead of it): no rise (< 5e-5 E0). No
handover was needed here. Note: rubble has no rest-overlap mechanism
(`RubbleEngine` does not implement `set_rest_overlaps`); if a trained model's placements
inject energy, the handover needs an engine-side change. The test stays as the check for
the trained model.

**GPU cost estimates, for the variant B / prior decision** (measured on the M3 Max; the GPU
was otherwise idle; indicative, not a benchmark):
- *Converged static solve for the call islands* (the Hertz-peak QS prior at P1's settings,
  f32 tolerance 1e-5): the existing GPU static solve with full settings
  (`statics_vs_reference.rs`) takes 9–10 ms for a 144-chunk slab (3 Newton, 373 CG) and
  167 ms for a 3,200-chunk slab (3 Newton, 1,262 CG); one island per workgroup, so
  islands run in parallel but each is latency-bound (≈ 0.03 ms per CG iteration at 144
  chunks, 0.13 ms at 3,200). Wall-sized calls (220–440 chunks): about 5–15 ms per frame
  with a call; a 10k-chunk building far more, unless the solve is spread over many groups.
- *A QS-frame branch per call frame* (the `blast_parity` frame of the same state: an engine
  step and the quasi-static cascade on a copy, then restored): the resident frame's own
  parts measure the engine step at 0.2–2 ms GPU per substep (CCD substeps multiply it on
  impact frames) and the cascade at 0.2 ms per pass (1 Newton × 16 CG, `blast_parity`'s
  budget; 2 passes), plus copies of the engine's bodies and contacts to snapshot and
  restore. About 1–4 ms per frame with calls, shared by all of the frame's calls (one
  branch for the batch); doubling the engine's cost on impact frames.

## Routing: the CPU's `select_islands` on the GPU (2026-10-22)

The planning session's decision: the learned path's calls must be the bodies the CPU's
routing puts in islands, from the same state. `learned_route.slang` (`learned_route.rs`)
ports stress-ref `World::select_islands`:
- a part per island (bounds of chunk centres plus or minus bounding radius, reach about the
  centre of mass, least plane-strain modulus, wave speed and half extent over its chunks,
  least tensile capacity over its bonds, struck when it has bonds) and per impactor
  (bounding sphere), each swept over dt;
- debris reach (`debris_reach_routing`);
- every overlapping pair, found by a bitonic sort on lower x and a sweep. The set is the
  CPU's, in any order: the components don't depend on it. Pairs join unless both bodies are
  immovable, or the pair is a slow cluster–cluster contact under tiered `tiered_fast_joins`.
  An owned pair whose rigid assumption fails (`hertz_duration`, round trip, capacity)
  seeds both bodies. The ground (a half-space) seeds only, and never under tiered;
- components by atomic-min label hooking and pointer jumping (48 rounds, convergence
  checked; the labels converge to each component's least index whatever the order). The
  routed bodies are the components holding a seed. All of them go in **one call per frame**
  (P1's call builder: `outcome_calls.rs`, "every routed chunk and impactor").

`STRESS_LEARNED_ROUTE=world` (the default) is the coupled world's routing: tiered under the
quasi-static solve, where only an impactor's fast contact seeds. That is the deployed
boundary and P0's closed-loop ceiling. `recorder` is a standalone world's routing,
untiered, as P1 first recorded: any contact whose rigid assumption fails seeds, fragments
meeting fragments and ground landings included. That routing isn't gated by the routing
check, so a batch is encoded every resident frame. The earlier rule (the punch's sweep,
calls per impactor group, the held-body join) remains as `STRESS_LEARNED_ROUTING=sweep`. It
has no CPU counterpart.

The two configurations differ a lot on P1's data (`stress-learned/tests/routing_configs.rs`,
4 evaluation recipes stepped by the recorder's world, 24 frames each). Only the strike frame
agrees. From frame 1 the untiered routing routes 6–33 clusters per frame with no impactor
(fragments touching fragments); the tiered one routes none of them.

**Parity** (`stress-rubble/tests/learned_routing.rs`). After every frame, the GPU batch's
routed set (its call's chunk and impactor keys) is compared with `predict_island_members`
on the synced CPU copy of the same state. Scenes: the M5 free wall and wall row, plus P1's
free- and anchored-wall evaluation recipes, under tiered and untiered routing, debris reach
off and on. Batches are dry (walls whole) or use the punch-like stub (walls fractured: the
hold's zeroed motion is given back to the CPU copy from `frozen`). **540/540 frames agree,
144 routed.**

Found and fixed on the way:
- the batch was encoded only when the *previous* frame's routing check flagged. The first
  resident frame after coupled ones takes that flag from the host's one-frame prediction,
  so a strike the CPU routes there was missed. With the CPU's routing, a batch is encoded
  every resident frame, its kernels gated by this frame's check;
- `learned_compact` and `learned_count_bonds` were gated by the routing check's flag, which
  untiered routing must not be;
- in learned mode the punch acted on islands no call held: under the old routing every
  island it would hit was in a call. With the CPU's routing those islands are the rigid
  engine's, as on the CPU, so the punch now takes only islands of calls that fell back
  (`FALLBACK_BIT`, `Punch::fallbacks_only`; off in the punch mode, so its digests are
  unchanged);
- window end: an island the decode broke bonds in but that did not split (one component)
  was released for a split that never came: frame-start pose, zero velocity.
  `learned_unsplit` places it by its fit after the split pass. The deformation test now
  checks that second window end too: v and ω within 1.3e-7 of P0's fit;
- features: `bmap`'s row map was never cleared between batches and was filled from the
  whole layout capacity. Stale records and old copies of split bonds made rows point at the
  wrong record. It is now cleared each batch (`feat_unmap`) and filled from live islands'
  bond ranges only; a row without a record is a bond cut by a split (broken). The cracked
  edge sets now match the builder after GPU fracture.

**Cost** (`STRESS_LEARNED_TIMING=1`, GPU timestamps per stage, Metal M-series; wall clock
loaded by another session's 8 CPU jobs). Stub network, `learned_p0 gpu --profile
blast_parity_routed --seconds 0.5`, `STRESS_GPU_FIXED_HORIZON=1 STRESS_DEBRIS_ROUTING=1`,
mean GPU ms per resident frame (28 frames):

| stage | free_wall_late2 | wall_row_late2 |
|---|---|---|
| route (`learned_route`, 195 passes) | 1.15 | 1.16 |
| select (`count_bonds`, `compact`) | 0.01 | 0.02 |
| gather | 0.01 | 0.01 |
| features (19 passes) | 0.25–0.27 | 2.48–2.67 |
| network (stub) and decode | 0.02 | 0.02 |
| apply (window end, 2 frames) | 0.28–0.34 | 0.36 |

Frame wall clock, median of frames 2..30 (loaded): learned 16–19 ms, `STRESS_IMPACT_PUNCH=gpu`
5.5–5.9 ms, no punch 3.2–3.7 / 9.8–11.2 ms. The learned path's excess is mostly host
encoding: about 230 passes, each with its own bind group (and uniforms), are encoded every
resident frame, since the CPU's routing batches every one. The route's 1.15 ms is fixed
cost: most passes are dispatched with nothing to do (the gate closed, the label rounds
converged). These are lossless to cut: indirect dispatch arguments zeroed by the gate and by
convergence, a group-local bitonic for the short strides, bind groups and uniforms kept
across frames, a parallel `learned_compact`, and the structure contacts off one thread.

At scale (`tests/learned_route_cost.rs`): the free wall struck beside 5,000 resting bonded
two-chunk bodies (10,220 chunks, 5,001 islands). Route 2.0 ms per frame in either
configuration (209 passes), `count_bonds` plus the single-thread `compact` 3.2–4.2 ms,
features 1.5 ms. By frame 6 the batch exceeds its capacities (2,048 call islands, 4,096 call
chunks) and the frame fails with that error. **Both configurations route the whole field
into one call.** Tiered too: with `tiered_fast_joins` off (the default), every overlapping
pair joins, so the struck wall's component reaches through the resting contacts to every
piece; the CPU does the same. With `STRESS_TIERED_JOIN=fast` slow contacts between clusters
join nothing. Untiered also seeds fragment–fragment contacts directly: the broadphase is
the same kernels (included above); only its gate differs.

Still open: **feature parity after a GPU-native split**. The structure-contact edge sets
differ from the builder on the wall row's fifth frame (e.g. builder-only (61, 90)…(69, 98),
corner neighbours across the crack, not near-pair ties). The features test runs under
`STRESS_LEARNED_ROUTING=sweep`, where the batches are as before (11 calls, all columns
agree), until this is found.

## Not done yet

- **variant B's QS-frame features** (`*_qs_feat`: the `blast_parity` QS frame of the same
  state) and **`log_qs_hertz_util`**: written as 0 (unavailable in the engine). A variant B
  model needs an in-engine QS-frame branch before the call, or the engine serves variant A:
  a decision for P1/P2/the user.
- the network itself (P0's kernel; the interface is agreed: `Learned::set_network_outputs`,
  `encode_batch(.., network)`, the feature buffer's sections in `Features::layout`).
- the fallback gate's input-range check (`FB_RANGE`) against the training ranges.
- `feat_sc` and `feat_cracked` walk a call's pairs on one thread (O(n²) for structure
  contacts): fine for wall-sized calls, slow for 4096-chunk calls.

## Known differences

- In learned mode the punch that serves a fallen-back call runs after the frame's split pass
  and its own routing check (the punch alone runs before them). On the free wall with every
  call falling back: 21.97 m/s, 3 fragments, against 25.41 / 3 for `STRESS_IMPACT_PUNCH=gpu`.
- Held bodies are static at their frame-start poses for the call's frame. With the CPU's
  routing (the default) a body outside the call that meets them during that frame is not
  joined, as on the CPU: it meets a static body. The sweep routing (`STRESS_LEARNED_ROUTING=
  sweep`) joins it (`learned_debris`, counted as `joined`; `tests/learned_gpu_join.rs`).
- A call's islands that the cascade splits during the held frame make the call stale: it is
  not applied (logged), its bodies released with their frame-start motion.
- The impactor's orientation uses the engine body's rotation without a principal-axes
  correction (exact for the box and sphere impactors in use).

## Configuration

`STRESS_IMPACT_PUNCH=learned`; `STRESS_LEARNED_STUB=zero|punch|nonfinite`;
`STRESS_LEARNED_ENERGY_TOL`; capacities `STRESS_LEARNED_MAX_CALLS` (64),
`_MAX_NODES` (65536), `_MAX_BONDS` (262144), `_MAX_CALL_ISLANDS` (2048),
`_MAX_CALL_CHUNKS` (4096): a batch over any is an error naming the variable;
`STRESS_DEBRIS_ROUTING=1`; `STRESS_LEARNED_ROUTE=world|recorder`, `STRESS_LEARNED_ROUTING=
cpu|sweep`, `STRESS_LEARNED_MAX_ROUTE_PAIRS` (16 joining pairs per island slot: past it,
or labels not converged, the frame fails with an error); `STRESS_LEARNED_TIMING=1` (GPU
time per stage, a diagnostic read); `STRESS_LEARNED_TRACE=1` prints the control words each frame.
Needs the frame's GPU split passes (`STRESS_GPU_LAYOUT=1 STRESS_GPU_FRAME=1`, as
`blast_parity_routed` sets).
