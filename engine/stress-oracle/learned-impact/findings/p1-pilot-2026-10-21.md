## What a record holds

Per frame (1/60 s), until the stopping rule ends the event:

- **Inputs, before the step:** the full state, the routing's predicted islands, every
  impactor contact the routing sweep predicts (time, point, direction, speed, reduced mass,
  Hertz time and peak force, punch ratio), the punch's plug and bonds, cross-cluster near
  pairs, and the **QS prior**: a converged static solve of the oracle's bond model under
  each predicted contact's Hertz peak force. P0 noted this prior "does not exist in the
  engine"; it now does, on a copy, with no effect on the run.
- **Oracle outcome:** break time bin (8 per frame), peak utilization per bond, the frame-end
  state (each chunk's pose, velocity, angular velocity: the frame-end poses P0's ceiling
  needs), per-bond cumulative energy (joint dissipation, dashpot dissipation, stored),
  failure mode, impactor contact timing, energy ledger, phase tag.
- **Baselines on the same frame-start state:** `blast_parity`'s QS frame and the punch's,
  from the state folded to rigid bodies (P0's `reduce_to_rigid`). Both are CPU mirrors of
  the baselines' stress models under the oracle's own contact, not rubble's GPU rigid engine.
  `World::adopt_model` gives a world `blast_parity`'s model while keeping its state. A test
  checks the result is bit-identical to a world built under `blast_parity`.

The stress-ref hooks are read-only, and their tests show them bit-identical:
`step_frame_watched`, `predict_contacts` (`predict_punch` now built on it),
`predict_island_members` (standalone), `adopt_model`, and P0's `world_handoff`. One
diagnostic field was added: `RtBond::damped`, the per-bond dashpot dissipation.

## Stopping rule, as run

Stop when every impactor has stopped (absolutely or relative to the remnant) or left the
bounds, **and** no bond broke for 30 frames, **and** the kinetic energy left in the bounds
is under 1% of the input, or everything there sleeps. Without gravity, the event also stops
when no two bodies can meet before the cap. That test is exact for ballistic motion, using
bounding spheres about each centre of mass. Without it, coasting fragments would run every
free-wall event to the cap. The cap is 10 s, flagged `truncated`.

The 9 truncations are real continuing motion, not rule failures:

- 8 are the members of one ensemble: a free wall knocked into a spin, with the slow ram in
  its opening. They can still meet. Re-recorded under the final rule, member 0 stops at
  9.1 s.
- 1 is a ram sliding along a free wall's face at 0.07 m/s relative to it.

## Pilot runs

| Pilot | Events | Purpose | Finding |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 | 242 | Tiers 1–2, free and anchored, M5 | Pipeline valid; **no-break share 0–12%** |
| v2 | 238 | 12-thread scaling, ensembles, bulk-size walls, M5 | Cost model, ensembles complete (12/12); bulk sizes cost 10× |
| v3 | 80 | Lower speed floor | No-break still 5% / 15% |
| v4 | 80 | Explicit sub-threshold band (20% of first impactors at 0.1–1 m/s) | **No-break 18% (free), 28% (anchored)**: the plan's ~20% |

**The oracle breaks bonds far below the punch's threshold.** At 1–2 m/s, 88% of hits
broke something (for example, 60 bonds from a 268 kg ram at 1.3 m/s). So a speed drawn
from the punch's force/capacity ratio alone yields almost no no-break events. The
generator now draws 20% of first impactors from a sub-threshold band (exact share, by the
hypercube). The rest stay centred on the punch threshold.

Frames per phase over the pilot (raw, before any sampling): pre-contact 1%, contact 4%,
secondary 17%, separation 14%, settling 64%. The trainer's phase quotas
(`outcome_sampling.py`) rebalance this. They keep every transition frame and raise an error
rather than drop one when the budget is too small.

- **Cost scales with chunks × simulated time:** median 0.09 s per chunk-second (p90 0.16).
  The tail is anchored walls that topple under gravity: a 756-chunk wall pushed by a
  3.3 t ram at 3.4 m/s ran at about 15 s per frame.
- **The QS prior is up to 30% on free walls**: it is solved on every frame with a
  predicted contact, including a slow ram still touching the wall long after the strike.
- **The QS baseline branch is 29–67% of the cost.** It re-runs the frame's contact substeps
  under `blast_parity`, and on large anchored walls its static solves dominate.
- **Threads:** at 12 parallel jobs each event costs 13% more than at 8, for the same 80
  recipes. So 12 jobs give 1.33× the throughput of 8.

## Decisions this needs (not taken here)

1. **Wall size range and count for the first bulk set.** For example, tier-1 walls at pilot
   sizes (2–3 days, ≤ 0.35 TB), with a smaller bulk-size set (≈ 3k configurations,
   ≈ 2 days, ≈ 0.2 TB) for resolution and size generalisation.
2. **Disk.** Lossless options: compress the arrays (zstd; settling frames dominate and
   compress well), or move the store to another volume. Writing f16 would be lossy and is not
   proposed.
3. **The QS branch's cost.** It is needed for variants B and C on every frame. Recording it
   only on routed frames (variants A/B) would cut much of its 29–67% share. That is a choice of
   what to record, not a change to physics. It is your call because variant C needs it.
4. **Ensemble policy.** Ensembles on all val/test configurations as decided (3.24 runs per
   configuration), or fewer members (for example 4) for training-set ensembles.
