# P1 recorder revision 8: bytes per event (2026-10-22)

The user's policy (relayed by planning, 2026-10-22): the dataset is the ground truth and
only grows. Every frame is kept, and the recorder stores what a future slice could need.
Revision 8 adds:

- exact World restart snapshots at boundary 0, at every boundary whose frame routes an
  island, and at every 10th boundary;
- f64 copies of the state arrays;
- each break's exact f64 time;
- per-frame impactor contact impulse and peak force.

The contact cohorts (sticks, pair offsets) are inside the World snapshot. Revision 8 also
turns off thinning by default (`--thin` opts back in).

## Pilot

The pilot recorded 8 training events per family with no ensembles, seed 8801, at 8 threads,
into `runs/size-r8/` (the script is `runs/size-r8.sh`). The table comes from
`python -m learned_impact.outcome_sizes`. Sizes are on-disk compressed MB per event (mean),
each column cumulative.

The pilot wrote each snapshot as its own zstd frame. The recorder now writes every 10th
boundary as a self-contained keyframe and each snapshot in between as a zstd frame that uses
the previous snapshot as its dictionary (`delta_of`; lossless, decoded by
`outcome::load_snapshot`, and tested to resume bit for bit). The "+ snapshots" column is
that scheme, recomputed offline on the pilot's snapshots with the same zstd level 19. The
masonry chain was measured on 7 events and scaled to all 8.

Snapshots stored as independent zstd frames (the pilot's own files) would take 4.85,
4.80, 4.02, 8.23, 44.3, 160.6 and 0.35 MB per event, in the table's order. A snapshot is
about 6–7× smaller under zstd than its raw MessagePack.

## What drives the size

Most boundaries route an island: 70–100% of frames during contact and secondary motion.
So the "every routed boundary" rule stores nearly one snapshot per frame. A snapshot at
every 10th boundary only would take 0.64, 0.65, 0.41, 0.97, 5.0, 15.2 and 0.20 MB per
event. Any boundary in between still resumes exactly, by stepping at most 9 frames from
the previous snapshot. This is lossless as well, since the oracle is deterministic and the
recipe is stored. It is planning's and the user's call; nothing is decided here.

The f64 state copies add about 80% to today's arrays: 1.25 MB of the walls' 1.56 MB, and
22 MB of masonry's 24 MB.

## Projections

These cover revision 8 as implemented (routed and every 10th boundary, key+delta, f64).

- Bulk v1 recorded again at its counts (3,091 free t1, 852 anchored t1, 388 free t2) would
  take about 17.3 + 4.8 + 1.3 = **23.4 GB**. Today it takes about 7.1 GB.
- Per 1,000 events: walls 3.4–5.7 GB, cuboid house 36 GB, masonry house about 97 GB,
  arch 1.2 GB.

## Wall time

Recording overhead is the share of wall time spent on state capture, routing prediction, IO
and snapshot compression. Revision 8 measured 17–28% on walls and 10–17% on stage-2. The
bulk records (revision ≤ 6) don't break out overhead, so the snapshot share isn't isolated
yet. Masonry houses take about 54 min of wall time per event at 8 parallel; one of the 8
hit the 10 s cap (truncated).

## Status

- The queue is still paused (`runs/PAUSE`), waiting on the user's disk budget. It sits at
  `evalplus-test-anchored-t2` 56/120.
- The paused process is a revision-7 binary. Resuming under revision 8 means restarting
  the queue with `runs/bin-r8/`, the integrated build: detached 098c1d3d plus every P1
  commit, with goldens and the snapshot tests passing.
- A resumed job keeps its plan. Only storage may change, and finished events keep theirs.
