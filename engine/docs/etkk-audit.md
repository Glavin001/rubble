# The ETK K-series on Rubble: an audit, and the comparison harness

Added 2026-11-06 (branch `feat/vehicle-compare`). Every number below is from
the harness (`engine/docs/measurements/vehicle-compare.md` and `.json`,
regenerated by the command below). The ETK (`etkk`, a community BeamNG mod,
config `kr6`, the soft-top roadster; `etkk_kc6` the hardtop coupe) is content
we measure, not content we ship: its files stay in the gitignored
`engine/crates/engine-webgpu/__mods/`.

**What it is for.** The generated car (gen2) should be as good as, or better
than, the ETK. The findings are written as design goals for the generator.

## The harness

```bash
cargo run --release -p engine-softbody --example vehicle_compare            # every manifest car, about 1 min
cargo run --release -p engine-softbody --example vehicle_compare -- --cars etkk,gen2 [--quick] [--out DIR]
node --experimental-websocket engine/crates/engine-webgpu/crash-lab-compare.mjs --cars sedan1,etkk --preset wall56
```

- **Cars: `engine/crates/engine-webgpu/vehicle-compare-cars.json`.** The CPU
  harness, the recorder (`crash-lab-record.mjs --car <name>`) and the
  side-by-side recorder (`crash-lab-compare.mjs`) all read it. gen2 already has
  its entry: `{ "name": "gen2", "mod": "gen2", "vehicle": "gen2", "pc": "gen2.pc", "optional": true }`.
  It runs as soon as `__mods/gen2/gen2/*.jbeam` exists. If the generator uses
  other names, edit that one line.
- **Deterministic.** The CPU reference runs one scene a thread. Two runs give
  byte-identical JSON, apart from the wall-clock fields.
- **Node sets come from geometry, never from node names.** Each car is turned
  to face +Z and centred, then settled 1 s. The skin box, the axles and the
  ground define the occupant points, the struck sides, the roof and the sills.
  A new car therefore needs no code.
- **Physics path.**
  - Rest runs on raycast wheels, as the page drives a car. A JBeam car runs it
    twice: in JBeam mode, and through the page's default `.truck` export.
  - Crash scenes run on each car's reference spawn: `.truck` soft tyres for the
    authored car, JBeam mode on pressure wheels for a JBeam car.
    `showcase_car.rs` uses the same spawns, so sedan1's numbers cross-check its
    documented ones. Cross-checked: residual crush 636 mm against 604–644,
    b1 22.1 against 22.9–23.6, door 10.0 / 12.5 / 27.8 against
    9.9 / 12.7 / 22.4 kN.
- **Scenes.**
  - rest: 10 s;
  - wall: 56 km/h, full width, 1 s after contact;
  - FMVSS 214 oblique pole: 32 km/h, 75°, 254 mm, at the driver's head;
  - pole sweep: 32 km/h, 90°, every 0.2 m from bumper to bumper, 0.8 s each;
  - FMVSS 214 MDB: the crash lab's barrier and placement, two-body contact, 1.5 s;
  - FMVSS 216a roof platen;
  - FMVSS 214 static door crush.

  The rigs are the reference scenes' own. The ram rig is now
  `crash_rigs::ram_rig`, and the MDB is `crash_rigs::mdb214_jbeam`.
- **Metrics** (definitions are in `src/vehicle_compare.rs`):
  - **Census:** nodes, node mass, spring ends per node, k·L, ε_y, and the share
    of nodes at the step's bound.
  - **Rest:** breaks, creep and residual kinetic energy.
  - **Settle drift:** each part's move about the body over the 1 s settle.
  - **Pulse:** the rear cabin's mass-weighted motion, 100 Hz.
  - **Crush:** NHTSA's six-zone crush at bumper height, in the cabin's
    rigid-fit frame. Campbell b1.
  - **Springback:** (dynamic − residual max) / dynamic.
  - **Wall force:** −dP/dt, at 100 Hz.
  - **Energy:** the world energy ledger.
  - **Panel ripple:** the RMS of √2·σ about each panel's rigid fit. A panel is
    a part with at least half its nodes on the skin.
  - **Survival-space intrusion:** at five occupant points, the most any node
    0.1–0.6 m away moved toward the point. **Choice:** the points are a
    mid-size package scaled by the wheelbase.
  - **Penetration into rigid colliders:** the deepest node, beam segment and
    collision-triangle point inside the wall or pole, sampled every 1 ms; the
    max and the p99. Vertical cylinders are tested exactly, boxes on 17 points
    a beam and 28 a triangle. Nodes with collision off are excluded.
  - **Damage:** broken hardware (hinges, latches, JBeam break groups) and
    detached parts (connectivity).
  - **Render mesh against its settled rest** (wheels excluded):
    - principal stretches;
    - newly flipped triangles;
    - torn or shattered vertices;
    - seams: boundary vertices of two pieces within 1 cm at rest, and how far
      they opened;
    - faceting: the change of the dihedral angle across welded interior edges.

    Self-intersection is not measured, because it is not cheap at 75k to 344k
    triangles.
- **Video:** `crash-lab-compare.mjs` records each car in turn with
  `crash-lab-record.mjs`: the same preset, camera and timing, and a label. It
  stacks them side by side with ffmpeg. There is one GPU job at a time, with a
  kill timer per car.

## Findings: the ETK's shortcomings (with numbers)

Bands are from `crash-reference-targets.md` and `vehicle-test-strategy.md`.

1. **Its lids are not held on Rubble.** Over the 1 s settle, the hood's nodes
   move 945 mm and the trunk's 574 mm about the body, in both configs.
   - The lids latch with BeamNG couplers (`couplerTag`, `couplerStrength`: 8
     nodes), which Rubble does not simulate. The hood starts with 401 N upward
     on `h3ll` and opens at about 0.9 m/s.
   - Every scene therefore runs with the lids open, and the hood is the worst
     panel for ripple (12.4 mm).
   - Coupler support is a solver gap, reported here rather than fixed.
2. **Parked: it is stable in JBeam mode, and unstable on the page's default
   path.**
   - **JBeam mode on raycast wheels:** 0 broken and 0 bent in 10 s. It never
     comes fully to rest: 0.5 J and 0.15 m/s at 10 s, against sedan1's 0 J and
     0.001 m/s.
   - **The page's `.truck` export:** the first break comes at 11 ms. After 10 s
     there are 10 broken and 131–143 bent beams, 2.4 kJ of kinetic energy, and
     a node at 10.9 m/s. The breaks are in `etkk_suspension_F`, at the
     suspension-to-body joint, and engine-to-body.
   - The roughly 150 beams seen breaking while parked are this export path,
     not the car itself. Load it with `jbeamMode=native`.
3. **Frontal: too stiff and too short.**

   | Measure | ETK | Band |
   |---|---|---|
   | dynamic crush | 511 mm | 620–750 |
   | t_m | 50 ms | 63–76 |
   | peak | 56 g | 34–47 |
   | residual crush, max | 281 mm | 380–580 |
   | b1 | 57.6 /s | 22.9–39.2 |
   | springback | 0.45 | about 0.25 |
   | restitution | 0.26 | 0.13–0.20 |
   | peak wall force | 651 kN at 28 ms | |

   For comparison, sedan1 has dynamic crush 813 mm, peak 35.8 g and
   restitution 0.18. Like BeamNG's BX, the ETK over-rebounds. Part of the cause
   is that it is a 3.92 m compact with a 2.39 m wheelbase.
4. **Side: the pole slips through it.**
   - **Oblique pole.** The skin at the pole crushes 512 mm residual (kr6) and
     58 mm (kc6), against a band of 297–445. But the pole's axis reaches
     969 mm and 1,561 mm past the struck skin, into a 1.875 m-wide car. The
     few skin nodes on the pole's line (8 of them) barely move while the pole
     passes between them.
   - **Pole sweep at 90°.** Nothing passes fully through. At mid-car (0.38–0.74
     of the length from the rear), the pole reaches 1.16–1.6 m (kr6) and up to
     1.78 m (kc6). sedan1's worst offset reaches 0.84 m.
   - **Penetration.** Beams and collision triangles reach the full pole radius
     (127 mm) in nearly every run. Nodes reach 36–58 mm. This is node-only
     contact, and sedan1 shows the same beam and triangle figures.
   - **MDB.** Residual crush is 210 mm for kr6 (band 126–206) and 392 mm for
     kc6. 29 beams break, including both left door hinges (`doorhinge_a_L`,
     `doorhinge_b_L`) and the door glass.
   - **Static door crush** fails all three phases: 5.4 / 10.1 / 19.5 kN,
     against ≥ 10.0 / 15.6 / 27.4.
5. **Roof is out of band both ways.** The hardtop (kc6) is 9.62 × its weight,
   against 4.48–5.32. The soft top (kr6) is 1.61 ×, carried by its roll hoop
   (`stb*`).
6. **Census.**

   | Measure | ETK | sedan1 |
   |---|---|---|
   | nodes, tyres excluded | 467 | 914 |
   | node mass p50 | 1.5 kg | 1.0 kg |
   | spring ends per node | 11 | 12 |
   | beam length p50 | 0.42 m | 0.33 m |
   | k·L p50 | 3.7e5 N (2.2 ×) | 1.7e5 N |
   | k·L p90 | 2.27e6 N (4.3 ×) | |
   | nodes at the bound | 65 % | 77 % |
   | collision triangles | 519 | 944 |
   | centre of mass height | 0.40 m | 0.51 m |

   Its quality comes from heavier nodes and longer beams (k·L ∝ m·L/n). The
   cost is sparse contact, which is finding 4.
7. **Interior.** The only interior structure is the two front seats (a few
   nodes each). There is no instrument panel, cross-car beam or console.
   Frontal intrusion, feet / knees / chest: ETK 61 / 105 / 71 mm, sedan1
   187 / 99 / 68 mm. Side MDB, hip / chest: ETK 372 / 463 mm, sedan1
   450 / 416 mm.
8. **Ripple after the wall.**
   - At 0.5–0.6 s the median panel is 0.8 mm (sedan1 2.0). The worst panels
     are the open hood and the suspension arms, at 10–12 mm.
   - At 1 s, the arms are still at 7.6–10 mm. sedan1's worst panel is 1.5 mm.
9. **Visual: the ETK's render mesh deforms far more cleanly than sedan1's.**
   At the pole the two crush about alike (512 against 452 mm residual):

   | Measure (side pole) | ETK | sedan1 |
   |---|---|---|
   | dihedral change p99 | 14.6° | 85° |
   | seam pairs opened > 5 cm | 0 | 1,069 |

   In the wall (the ETK crushes less):

   | Measure (wall) | ETK | sedan1 |
   |---|---|---|
   | stretch p99 | 1.55 | 13.2 |
   | triangles stretched > 1.5 × | 852 | 24,529 |
   | new flipped triangles | 189 | 7,852 |
   | seams opened > 5 cm | 0 of 785 pairs | 1,297 of 11,569 |
   | torn vertices | 0.9 % | 0.6 % |

   The ETK still has faceting: 418 edges change by more than 60° in the wall,
   and up to 177°. Its 75k render triangles ride 519 collision triangles.
10. **Cost.** The ETK is 0.6–2.5 s of wall clock per simulated second on the
    CPU reference (rest, wall, MDB); sedan1 is 1.4–7.4. That follows the node
    counts.

11. **On the page (GPU, `jbeamMode=native`) the same wall looks far worse
    than on the CPU.** This is observed in the side-by-side video, not
    measured.
    - Within about 250 ms of contact, the ETK sheds many fragments and its
      front tears open. By 2 s it is a crumpled heap.
    - The CPU reference breaks only 2 beams in the same scene.
    - Its wheels are not drawn on the page.
    - Its impact detection fires at 10 ms, before it reaches the wall. The
      recorder aligns the clips with `--impact`.
    - The GPU path has no harness yet, so this is a follow-up for the solver
      and page owners.

    The video is at
    `engine/crates/engine-webgpu/recordings/compare/wall56.mp4` (gitignored:
    it shows the mod's mesh). Regenerate it with `crash-lab-compare.mjs`.

## Design goals for gen2 (from the findings)

- **Lids:** latch hood and trunk with break-group beams, not couplers (finding 1).
- **Rest:** zero breaks and zero bent beams, on both the JBeam-mode and the
  `.truck`-export raycast paths; under 0.01 m/s after 10 s (finding 2).
- **Frontal:** inside the bands for dynamic crush (620–750 mm), t_m, peak,
  residual (380–580 mm), b1, springback (about 0.25) and restitution
  (0.13–0.20). The ETK misses all seven (finding 3).
- **Side:** the pole's reach should be about the residual crush (297–445 mm),
  not 1–1.6 m (finding 4).
  - Keep the skin's lanes at or under the 254 mm pole; the ETK's beams are
    0.42 m.
  - MDB residual 126–206 mm.
  - Door crush ≥ 10 / 15.6 / 27.4 kN.
- **Roof:** 4.48–5.32 × weight (finding 5).
- **Stiffness:** reach the ETK's k·L (3.7e5 N p50) with heavier structural
  nodes on long beams. Add light contact nodes in the lanes, so the pole
  goal still holds (finding 6).
- **Interior:** an instrument-panel cross beam, a console and seats, so that
  intrusion at feet and knees stays under the firewall band (1–67 mm)
  (finding 7).
- **Ripple:** under 1 mm by 1 s (finding 8).
- **Visual:** at least the ETK's mesh quality (finding 9):
  - dihedral change p99 ≤ 15° at the pole;
  - 0 seams opened past 5 cm;
  - stretch p99 ≤ 1.6;
  - flipped triangles in the hundreds, not the thousands.

  Bind each vertex to its own part's nearby nodes (flexbody-like), not across
  shut lines.
- **Penetration into rigid colliders** is the opt-in triangle contact's
  measure. Today beams and triangles reach the full pole radius (127 mm) on
  every car.

## Caveats

- The harness frame, occupant points and roof, sill and door picks are
  geometric Choices. They are documented in the code.
- Pole crush at sparse skin nodes under-reads, so read it beside the pole's
  reach.
- The ETK's lids are open in every scene (finding 1).
- The ETK's ledger rises 14–16 J in the wall; sedan1's rises 0. The ledger
  does not cover pressure tyres or bounded beams (`vehicle-test-strategy.md`).
- Raycast wheels are used only at rest.

## Re-run with couplers and the contact fix (2026-11-06, `feat/jbeam-community`)

The harness again (`vehicle_compare --cars sedan1,etkk,etkk_kc6,ccf,vertex`) after
BeamNG's node couplers (spec §7.1), JBeam mode as the page's default for JBeam
content, and `feat/jbeam-features`' contact rest fix. What changed for the ETK:

| Finding | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Lids held | hood 945 mm, trunk 574 mm adrift over the settle | **resolved**: latched by their couplers; the largest settle drifts are the soft top 32.8 mm (kr6) and the front suspension 5 mm; the lids are not among them |
| 2. Parked | JBeam mode 0 / 0; `.truck` export 10 broken | JBeam mode 0 / 0 (0.5 J, 0.15 m/s); the `.truck` path is no longer the page's default |
| 3. Frontal (kr6) | dynamic 511 mm, t_m 50 ms, 56 g, residual 281 mm, e 0.26 | dynamic 489 mm, t_m 46 ms, 66 g, residual 228 mm, e 0.35 (stiffer with the lids latched) |
| 4. Side pole (kr6) | residual 512 mm, reach 969 mm | residual 57 mm, reach 1,045 mm (the pole still slips between the skin's nodes) |
| 4. MDB (kr6 / kc6) | 210 / 392 mm | 364 / 265 mm |
| 4. Door crush | 5.4 / 10.1 / 19.5 kN | 5.7 / 10.7 / 38.5 kN (initial and intermediate still under 10.0 / 15.6) |
| 5. Roof (kr6 / kc6) | 1.61 / 9.62 × | 6.14 / 9.79 × |

The CCF and the Vertex are in the table too (`vehicle-compare.md`). Two open points
from them: the Vertex's wall ledger shows a 27 kJ rise (the energy check fails; its
front suspension rings at 16–20 mm), and the CCF's wall residual crush reads 29 mm
with b1 2,335 /s, with both headlights detached (the cabin fit or the crush frame
is suspect, not yet read).
